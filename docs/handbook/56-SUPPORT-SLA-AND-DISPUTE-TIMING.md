# Support SLA and Dispute Timing

## 1. NEXUS support priority

Before a staffed support organization exists, NEXUS should not publish hard guaranteed response times that it cannot reliably meet.

Priority ordering still exists, with examples:

1. Critical malware/security incidents;
2. payment/access failures with broad impact;
3. active scam/abuse cases;
4. disputes/appeals;
5. ordinary account/product/community support.

Internal target times may be used for queue management without becoming contractual public SLA.

## 2. Seller support term

A seller may define how long support is included, for example:

- 30 days;
- 90 days;
- 365 days;
- another clearly displayed product-specific period.

This support duration is distinct from:

- license lifetime;
- update entitlement;
- NEXUS platform support;
- response-time guarantee.

Seller is not automatically required to promise a specific number of hours to first response.

## 3. Dispute timing

NEXUS staff may use internal target handling times and escalation rules.

A missed target does **not** automatically mean:

- buyer wins;
- seller wins;
- refund is issued;
- payout is released.

The outcome follows evidence, policy, provider/legal constraints and staff decision.

## 4. Subscription fairness

A paid plan may improve NEXUS support routing/priority only where advertised, but it must never buy:

- a favorable moderation verdict;
- a favorable dispute verdict;
- verification;
- removal of valid public sanctions;
- weaker malware/security review.
